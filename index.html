Proposal draft for discussion

Monitoring soil salinity under treated wastewater irrigation in Al Ain

Abu Dhabi is moving Al Ain farms from saline groundwater to treated wastewater, and no programme measures what the switch does to the soil, farm by farm or below the surface. This proposal maps root-zone salinity and soil moisture with NISAR L-band radar, measured soil permittivity and a radiative-transfer model, with machine learning used last.

QuestionIs the switch reclaiming saline farms, or adding salt where the treated water is itself saline?
ApproachPhysics first: lab permittivity and a two-layer RT model interpret NISAR backscatter.
OutputSeasonal salinity and moisture maps at three depths, with a farm-level early warning.

01 · Rationale: the need

Al Ain's farm soils are already past the limits of its hardiest crop

Salt builds up naturally in a climate with under 100 mm of rain a year, and decades of irrigation with saline groundwater have added to it. Average farm soils now sit two to four times above the salinity threshold of date palm.

02 · Rationale: the problem

The water is changing faster than anyone can measure the soil

More than 1,600 farms are moving to treated wastewater. That water is fresher than the wells it replaces, but it is not salt-free, and current monitoring cannot see what happens at farm scale, at root depth or over time.

Problem statement

Abu Dhabi is replacing saline groundwater with treated wastewater on thousands of Al Ain farms. Depending on water quality, soil texture and starting condition, the change could reverse salinisation or keep adding salt where the treated water carries its own, and current monitoring cannot see it at farm scale, at root-zone depth or over time. Without that evidence, regulators cannot verify the 2022 standard's "close monitoring" condition, and farmers have no independent proof of what the water is doing to their land.

03 · Research gaps and objectives

Seven gaps, one objective, four work streams

The gaps concern what is happening to soil on Al Ain farms and why radar has so far failed to see it. Hover or tap a sub-objective to light up the gaps it closes.

Main objective

To measure, map and attribute changes in root-zone soil salinity and soil moisture on Al Ain farms, including those switching from groundwater to treated wastewater, using NISAR L-band radar interpreted through measured soil permittivity, a radiative-transfer model and machine learning.

04 · What has been done

Twenty studies mapped salinity; almost none looked below the surface

Most published maps use optical indices and machine learning on topsoil. Radar studies are fewer and mostly C-band, and the physics-based ones show standard models breaking down in extremely arid soil. Filter by approach, then open a study.

05 · Suggested methodology

Physics first, machine learning second

Lab-measured permittivity fixes the dielectric model. A two-layer radiative-transfer model links NISAR backscatter to moisture, salinity, texture and a subsurface layer. A hybrid model then maps salinity by depth, and an impact design attributes change to the switch. Click any box.

08 · The big picture

From one farm's soil to the nation's water strategy

Every national water target depends on treated wastewater being safe to put on farmland at scale. This project supplies the soil evidence that each level needs, from a farmer deciding where to leach to the federal reporting on SDG targets.

Farm

Know the soil

Root-zone salinity and moisture per field: improving or not, where to leach, which crops fit.

Emirate

Monitor the rollout

Every farm covered, not a sample: where reuse reclaims land and which farms to connect next.

Nation

Meet the targets

Evidence behind 95% reuse, 100% recycled-water use and sustainable local food production.

Global

Report on the SDGs

Safe water reuse (SDG 6.3) and land degradation neutrality (SDG 15.3).
